Symbols and formulas

On this page

  1. Why
  2. Rule
  3. Example
  4. Practice

Why

Chemists do not write “water consists of hydrogen and oxygen”, they write H₂O. Symbols and formulas are a shorthand that is read the same way all over the world.

Rule

Compound

A compound consists of atoms of different elements joined in a fixed ratio, for example water or carbon dioxide.

Molecule

A molecule is a small particle made of several joined atoms, for example a water molecule.

Formula

A formula uses element symbols to show which atoms a substance is made of. A small subscript number, the index, tells how many atoms there are.

The formula of water is H2O: one molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms (H) and 1 oxygen atom (O). If there is no index, it is 1 atom.

Some formulas and their atoms
SubstanceFormulaAtoms in one particle
waterH2O2 H, 1 O
carbon dioxideCO21 C, 2 O
methaneCH41 C, 4 H
ammoniaNH31 N, 3 H
oxygenO22 O

Example

Reading the formula of methane

Methane has the formula CH4. C stands for carbon, H for hydrogen. The 4 says: for 1 carbon atom there are 4 hydrogen atoms. A methane molecule has 5 atoms in total.

    Hint

    The symbol has one or two letters and is in the table of the periodic table.

    Solution

Exercise

How many hydrogen atoms does one particle of ammonia with the formula NH₃ have?

Right answer: 3

The index 3 is after the H. It applies only to the H, so there are 3 hydrogen atoms and 1 nitrogen atom.
