Chemical reactions

On this page

  1. Why
  2. Rule
  3. Example
  4. Practice

Why

When ice melts, it is still water. When wood burns, no wood is left. There is a big difference between these two processes, and this page helps you to spot it.

Rule

Chemical reaction

In a chemical reaction substances turn into other substances. The new substances have different properties from the old ones.

Change of state

In a change of state such as melting or evaporating the substance stays the same. Only the state of matter changes.

Hints of a chemical reaction are for example a change of colour, a rising gas, heat or light. Only one thing is certain: a new substance is formed.

In the particle model the atoms rearrange in a reaction. The atoms themselves are kept: they do not disappear and no new ones are created.

Example

Hydrogen burns

Hydrogen and oxygen react to form water. Two gases have become a new substance with different properties. That is a chemical reaction. When water evaporates, however, the substance is still water. That is only a change of state.

Practice

Exercise

Which of these processes are chemical reactions?

  • Wood burns. right answer

    Burning forms new substances, for example carbon dioxide and ash. It is a chemical reaction.

  • Hydrogen burns to water. right answer

    Hydrogen and oxygen become water, a new substance. It is a chemical reaction.

  • Ice melts.

    Melted ice is still water. Only the state changes.

  • Water evaporates.

    Water vapour is still water. It is only a change of state.

When melting and evaporating the substance stays the same, only the state changes. When burning, new substances are formed.

In a chemical reaction new atoms are created.

Right answer: The statement is false.

Exercise

How can you be sure that a chemical reaction has taken place?

  • A new substance is formed. right answer

    That is the certain sign: the substances have turned into other substances.

  • The substance gets warmer.

    A substance can also get warmer without reacting, for example on a radiator.

  • The substance changes its state of matter.

    That is a change of state. The substance stays the same.

Only a new substance shows a reaction for sure. Heat or a change of state is not enough.
