Why

Why does water flow, but a stone does not? Why does air fill a whole room? We cannot see the smallest parts of substances. With a model we imagine them and use it to explain what we observe.

Rule

Particle model

In the particle model every substance consists of very small particles, which you imagine as balls.

The three states of matter in the particle model
StateArrangement of the particlesMovement
solidclose together, in ordervibrate in place
liquidclose together, not in orderchange places
gaseousfar apartmove freely through the space

When a substance gets warmer, its particles move faster. With enough heat they break out of their fixed order: the substance melts. With even more heat they fly apart: the substance boils.

Example

Water is heated

Ice is solid. The water particles sit close together. At 0 °C it melts, then they are mobile and the water flows. At 100 °C it boils: the particles fly apart and it is water vapour.

solid: The particles sit close together and vibrate in place.

A box with 30 particles and a slider for the temperature. At a low temperature (solid) the particles sit close together and vibrate in place. At a medium temperature (liquid) they are close together and change places. At a high temperature (gaseous) they are far apart and move freely through the whole box.

Exercise

Match each state with the fitting description in the particle model.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: solid – particles close together, vibrate in place; liquid – particles close together, change places; gaseous – particles far apart, free to move

The higher the temperature, the more mobile the particles. In the solid state they have fixed places, in the liquid state they change places, in the gaseous state they fly freely.

Why does water flow?
