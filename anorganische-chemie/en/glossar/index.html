Glossary

The terms of this app, from A to Z, each with the page that explains it.

Atomic number

The atomic number tells how many protons an atom has. Every element has its own atomic number.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Boiling point

The temperature at which a liquid becomes gaseous. For a pure substance it is always the same.

Explained on the page Properties of substances

Change of state

In a change of state such as melting or evaporating the substance stays the same. Only the state of matter changes.

Explained on the page Chemical reactions

Chemical reaction

In a chemical reaction substances turn into other substances. The new substances have different properties from the old ones.

Explained on the page Chemical reactions

Coefficient

A coefficient is the big number in front of a formula. It tells how often the formula occurs.

Explained on the page Reaction equations

Compound

A compound consists of atoms of different elements joined in a fixed ratio, for example water or carbon dioxide.

Explained on the page Symbols and formulas

Electron

An electron is a particle in the electron shell with a negative charge.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Element symbol

The element symbol is the abbreviation of an element with one or two letters, for example C for carbon.

Explained on the page Reading the periodic table

Formula

A formula uses element symbols to show which atoms a substance is made of. A small subscript number, the index, tells how many atoms there are.

Explained on the page Symbols and formulas

Group

A group is a column in the periodic table. Elements in one group have similar properties.

Explained on the page Reading the periodic table

Main group

The eight main groups are groups 1, 2 and 13 to 18 of the periodic table. They are numbered I to VIII.

Explained on the page The eight main groups

Mass number

The mass number is the number of protons and neutrons together.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Melting point

The temperature at which a solid becomes liquid. For a pure substance it is always the same.

Explained on the page Properties of substances

Metal

Metals conduct electricity and heat well, can be formed into sheets and wires and have a metallic lustre.

Explained on the page Metals and non-metals

Metalloid

Metalloids stand in the periodic table between metals and non-metals and cannot clearly be put into either group. Examples are boron and silicon.

Explained on the page Metals and non-metals

Mixture

A mixture consists of several substances, for example air, milk or granite.

Explained on the page Pure substances and mixtures

Molecule

A molecule is a small particle made of several joined atoms, for example a water molecule.

Explained on the page Symbols and formulas

Neutron

A neutron is a particle in the nucleus with no charge.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Non-metal

Non-metals do not have these metal properties. Examples are hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur.

Explained on the page Metals and non-metals

Nucleus

The nucleus is in the centre of the atom. It consists of protons and neutrons.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Particle model

In the particle model every substance consists of very small particles, which you imagine as balls.

Explained on the page Particle model

Period

A period is a row in the periodic table. The number of the period tells how many shells an atom has.

Explained on the page Reading the periodic table

Proton

A proton is a particle in the nucleus with a positive charge.

Explained on the page Atomic structure

Pure substance

A pure substance consists of only one kind of substance, for example pure water or pure oxygen.

Explained on the page Pure substances and mixtures

Reaction equation

A reaction equation describes a reaction with formulas. The starting substances are on the left of the arrow, the products on the right.

Explained on the page Reaction equations
