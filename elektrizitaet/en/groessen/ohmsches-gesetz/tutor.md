# Ohm's law – tutor file

### ohm-labor (interactive exercise OhmLabor)

- Link: [./#ohm-labor](./#ohm-labor), in the section „Practice“
- What to do: You set voltages on a battery, record measuring points in a plot and give the unknown resistance.
- Answer format: At least three measuring points at different voltages above 0 V (slider, then “Record a measuring point”) and the resistance in Ω as a number; then “Check”.
- What counts as right: Three different voltages above 0 V are measured, and the resistance given is at most 0.5 Ω away from the true one. Which voltages you choose does not matter.
- Typical mistakes and the app's message:
  - Fewer than three measuring points, or the same voltage several times, or 0 V.
  - No resistance entered.
  - Calculated voltage divided by current the wrong way round (I : U instead of U : I) or did not convert the current from mA.

### og-doppelt (true-false)

- Link: [./#og-doppelt](./#og-doppelt), in the section „Practice“
- Task: You double the voltage across an ohmic resistor. Then the current doubles too.
- Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
- Solution: The statement is true.
- Explanation: Because the ratio $\frac{U}{I}$ stays the same, the current grows in step with the voltage. The characteristic curve is a straight line through the origin.
