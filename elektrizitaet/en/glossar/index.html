Glossary

The terms of this app, from A to Z, each with the page that explains it.

Ammeter

An ammeter measures the current. You put it into the circuit so that the current flows through the device.

Explained on the page Current

Characteristic curve

A characteristic curve shows how the current depends on the voltage. For an ohmic resistor it is a straight line through the origin.

Explained on the page Ohm's law

Circuit

A circuit is a closed path for the electric current, from the power source through the devices and back again.

Explained on the page The simple circuit

Circuit diagram

A circuit diagram shows a circuit with circuit symbols instead of drawings of the parts.

Explained on the page Switches and circuit symbols

Conductor

A conductor lets electric current through well. All metals are conductors, for example copper, iron and aluminium.

Explained on the page Conductors and insulators

Current

The current says how much electric current flows in a circuit. Its symbol is I and it is measured in amperes (A).

Explained on the page Current

Fuse

A fuse breaks the circuit automatically when too much current flows, for example in a short circuit. This protects against fires.

Explained on the page Staying safe with electricity

Insulator

An insulator (also called a non-conductor) hardly lets any electric current through, for example plastic, rubber, glass, wood and porcelain.

Explained on the page Conductors and insulators

Ohm's law

For an ohmic resistor the voltage divided by the current is always the same: R=UI. The resistance does not change when you change the voltage.

Explained on the page Ohm's law

Parallel circuit

In a parallel circuit the parts are connected next to each other. The current has several paths, one for each part.

Explained on the page Parallel circuit

Resistance

The electrical resistance says how strongly a part hinders the current. Its symbol is R and it is measured in ohms (Ω).

Explained on the page Resistance

Series circuit

In a series circuit the parts are connected one behind the other. There is only one path for the current.

Explained on the page Series circuit

Short circuit

In a short circuit the current flows along a path with no load at all. The cable can get very hot, so a short circuit is dangerous.

Explained on the page Parallel circuit

Switch

A switch opens or closes the circuit. Closed, the current flows; open, none flows.

Explained on the page Switches and circuit symbols

Total resistance

The total resistance is the resistance the whole circuit presents to the source. In a series circuit it is the sum of the single resistances.

Explained on the page Series circuit with meters

Voltage

The voltage says how strongly the power source drives the current. Its symbol is U and it is measured in volts (V).

Explained on the page Voltage

Voltmeter

A voltmeter measures the voltage. You connect it in parallel: with its two terminals at the two ends of the part you want to measure.

Explained on the page Voltage
