Conductors and insulators

On this page

  1. The idea
  2. What to remember
  3. Example
  4. Practice

The idea

Inside, a cable is made of copper; outside, of plastic. Why not all copper or all plastic? Because some materials let current through and others do not.

What to remember

Conductor

A conductor lets electric current through well. All metals are conductors, for example copper, iron and aluminium.

Insulator

An insulator (also called a non-conductor) hardly lets any electric current through, for example plastic, rubber, glass, wood and porcelain.

Important materials and whether they conduct electricity
MaterialConductor or insulator?
CopperConductor
IronConductor
AluminiumConductor
Graphite (pencil lead)Conductor
PlasticInsulator
RubberInsulator
GlassInsulator
Wood (dry)Insulator

Water can conduct, too

Tap water and salt water conduct electricity because substances are dissolved in them. That is why wet hands and electricity are a dangerous mix. More on the page “Staying safe”.

Example

Why a cable has two parts

The copper wire inside is a conductor: the current flows in it. The sheath of plastic is an insulator: it protects you when you touch the cable and stops two wires from touching each other.

Practice

Exercise

Which materials are conductors?

  • Copper right answer

    Copper is a metal and conducts electricity very well. That is why cables are made of copper inside.

  • Iron right answer

    Iron is a metal too and conducts electricity.

  • Rubber

    Rubber does not conduct electricity. That is why it is suitable as a sheath around cables and on handles.

  • Glass

    Glass does not conduct electricity and is an insulator.

All metals are conductors. Rubber and glass are insulators.

Exercise

What is the sheath of a cable, which protects you, made of?

  • Plastic, an insulator right answer

    Plastic does not let current through. That is why you can touch the sheath safely.

  • Copper, a conductor

    Copper is the wire inside. It conducts the current and would be dangerous as a sheath.

  • Iron, a conductor

    Iron conducts electricity and is not suitable as a protective sheath.

The sheath must be an insulator so that the current flows only in the wire and you are protected.
