Textcheck

Here you practise careful reading. You see an original text and a variant of it. The variant hides errors. Find them and mark them. For pupils from grade 3 to the Abitur in the German school system. The texts and the errors get harder with the grade.

How it works

  • Read the original. It is correct and names its source.
  • Read the variant and mark the words that are wrong. You mark with a click or a tap; with the keyboard you choose words with the arrow keys and mark with the space bar.
  • Tap "Check". You see which errors you found, which you missed, and why they are wrong.
  • Every text has three ways to practise. In the first you see the original. With "the source as PDF" you open the source as a PDF and compare. With "Spot the original" you see two texts, A and B: you choose the text with the errors and mark them there.
  • It is fine to mark a few extra words if they stand right next to a long error in the same sentence.

What you learn

  • I can find misspelt words in a short text and mark them.
  • I can mark places where the variant contradicts the original.
  • I can find sentences that contradict each other in the same text.
  • I can compare a wrong time, place or amount with the original.
  • I can find misspelt words, even ones a spell checker does not find.
  • I can tell an opinion from a fact and mark the opinion.
  • I can recognise a conclusion that the text does not support.
  • I can recognise judging or emotional wording.
  • I can compare a text with its source and find places that differ from the source.
  • I can compare two texts and tell which one has errors.

How this exercise is grounded

Here we say what we rely on and what we worked out ourselves. Documented means: a source we read names this kind of error. Derived means: our own conclusion from related sources. From which grade a kind of error occurs is our own setting. None of the sources we read assigns the kinds of error to grades.

The kinds of error:

  • Spelling (rule-based mistake), documented, from grade 3: IQB learning task and handbook, KMK standards.
  • Slip of the pen, documented, from grade 3: Schmellentin and Lindauer (2019).
  • Mistake with a real word (their/there, its/it's), documented, from grade 5: IQB handbook and learning task.
  • Punctuation, derived, from grade 3: KMK standards, Schmellentin and Lindauer.
  • Number, name, date or place changed, derived, from grade 3: IGLU 2021, KMK standards.
  • Wrong fact, derived, from grade 5: IGLU 2021, Cook, Ellerton and Kinkead (2018).
  • Added statement, derived, from grade 5: Voss, Carstensen and Bos (2005).
  • Connective or negation changed, derived, from grade 5: Voss, Carstensen and Bos (2005).
  • Contradiction in the text, derived, from grade 5: Voss, Carstensen and Bos (2005), KMK standards.
  • Amount or degree changed, derived, from grade 7: our own inference, no source read.
  • Overgeneralisation, derived, from grade 7: Cook, Ellerton and Kinkead (2018).
  • Faulty conclusion or cause, derived, from grade 7: Cook, Ellerton and Kinkead (2018), IGLU 2021.
  • Opinion presented as fact, derived, from grade 7: IGLU 2021, Voss, Carstensen and Bos (2005), KMK standards.
  • False authority or source, derived, from grade 10: Cook, Lewandowsky and Ecker (2017).
  • Loaded, emotional wording, derived, from grade 10: IGLU 2021, KMK standards, Cook and others (2017).

What we cannot back up:

  • That this exercise improves reading skills. There is no study for it.
  • That finding errors in texts trains checking unknown web pages. That is something else (Wineburg and McGrew 2018).
  • The length and sentence structure of the texts for each grade: these are settings of the authors.
  • The spelling mistakes are constructed for the words of the texts. The kinds of mistake come from the IQB materials; we did not collect the individual misspellings of learners. In English texts we carried over the same kinds; this is our own adaptation and no source names it.
